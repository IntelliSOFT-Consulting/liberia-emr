# 0005: Cross-facility identity reconciliation: link, never merge

**Status:** Proposed, awaiting MOH ICT agreement (LE-22, target 21 August 2026)

## Context

Two facilities register the same person independently. Someone attends Careysburg in March
and Barnersville in September, and nothing at registration time tells either facility about
the other. Central receives both records and must decide whether they describe one person.

There is no existing key that answers the question. From
`content-liberia-national/…/patientidentifiertypes.csv`:

- **MOH Health Record Number** is mandatory, but its uniqueness behaviour is `LOCATION`,
  meaning facility-scoped. Both facilities will issue `00001` to different people. It cannot be the
  cross-facility key.
- **Liberia National ID** is globally `UNIQUE`, but it is `Required=false`. It is optional
  at registration and will frequently be absent.
- **ANC Number** is facility-scoped and programme-specific.

So the mandatory identifier is not unique nationally, and the unique identifier is not
mandatory. This follows from MOH registration policy, not from the EMR design, and the sync
layer cannot be built without a decision about it.

The decision is a **clinical safety** decision. Attaching one woman's obstetric history to
another is patient harm, and in the MCH programme the highest-risk false positive is
concrete rather than hypothetical: **twins** share surname, date of birth, sex, mother's
name, phone number and facility. Any rule that auto-matches on name plus date of birth
merges twins.

Getting it wrong in the other direction is also harmful, just quieter: an unmatched record
means a clinician sees no history for a patient who has one.

## Decision

**Central maintains identity by linking records, never by merging them.**

1. **Central Person Identifier (CPI).** Central assigns a CPI on first receipt of a
   patient. It is generated by central, never entered by a clinician, and not printed on a
   patient card in this release. It is central's stable key for a *person*, distinct from
   the facility keys it receives.

2. **Link, do not merge.** Facility records are stored exactly as received. A link table
   maps `(facility_code, patient_uuid) → CPI`. An incorrect link is corrected by editing
   one row; an incorrect merge destroys the evidence needed to detect it. This property is
   what makes an imperfect matcher safe to run in production.

3. **Three bands, not a threshold.** High confidence → auto-link. Middle → **human review
   queue**, with no link until a person decides. Low → new CPI. A matcher with only "match"
   and "no match" has chosen which direction to be silently wrong in.

4. **Deterministic first, gated.** An exact National ID match auto-links only if sex agrees
   and date of birth agrees within tolerance. A National ID that matches while sex
   disagrees is a transcription error or a shared ID; it goes to review.

5. **Probabilistic for the rest.** Fellegi–Sunter style scoring over normalised and
   phonetically encoded names, date of birth, sex, mother's name, phone, residence
   district, with frequency weighting so that a common surname counts for less than a rare
   one. A facility-scoped MOH HRN already linked to a different CPI at that facility is
   evidence *against* a match.

6. **Never auto-link on name + date of birth alone.** It goes to the review band. See the
   twins case above.

7. **`birthdate_estimated` is honoured.** An estimated date of birth carries materially
   less weight than a documented one, and estimated-agreeing-with-estimated least of all.
   Ignoring the flag manufactures confidence out of a clerk's best guess.

8. **Thresholds are configuration, tuned on real registration data during Sprint 3**,
   reviewed with the MOH ICT Unit and recorded. They are not code constants and not
   design-time guesses.

Two things must be settled by the MOH for this ADR to move from Proposed to Accepted:
whether a central identifier scheme is acceptable (versus making National ID mandatory at
registration), and **which named MOH role owns the review queue**, with an expected
turnaround. An unstaffed review queue is equivalent to no matching at all, at greater cost.

## Consequences

- Central holds two representations: the facility records as sent, and an identity layer
  over them. Every cross-facility read resolves through the link table.
- Duplicates are visible only at central. Facility staff cannot adjudicate a duplicate
  whose counterpart is at another facility, which is why the queue owner must be an MOH
  role.
- **A link made at central does not propagate back to the facility.** Sync is
  unidirectional; the facility keeps its own view and never learns of the link. Acceptable
  for Sprint 3, where nothing at the facility reads cross-facility data, and resolved at
  read time in Sprint 4. Reflecting links in a facility database would be a second write
  direction and needs its own ADR.
- The review queue is ongoing operational work for the life of the deployment, not a
  migration task. It should be resourced as such.
- **Facility-side merges reach central as ordinary sync events and must collapse the link.**
  When facility staff merge two of their own records, OpenMRS voids the losing record and
  re-points its data; sync replays all of it. The identity layer must then fold the losing
  record's `(facility_code, patient_uuid)` mapping into an alias of the surviving record's
  CPI, exactly as it does for its own reversed links. A facility merge that leaves two live
  CPI mappings behind recreates at central the duplicate the facility just resolved.
- The middle band will be larger than anyone expects while National ID remains optional.
  That is the honest cost of optional registration, surfaced rather than hidden inside an
  auto-match rule.
- Adding a third facility changes nothing structurally; this is the scaling template.
- Cross-facility query (Sprint 4) is unblocked, since it can now ask a well-formed question:
  "which records share this CPI?"
